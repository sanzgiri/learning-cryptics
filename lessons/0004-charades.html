Lesson 4 · Cryptic Crosswords

Charades

New trick, and a step up: instead of one wordplay operation, a charade clue chains several small ones together. Named for the party game — you build the answer piece by piece, each piece separately clued, glued together left to right in order.

The recipe

Per the Best For Puzzles tutorial, a charade clue breaks the answer into two or more parts. Each part gets its own short definition or synonym, and — unlike an anagram — there's no shuffling. You just read left to right and concatenate:

  • Definition — same as always, start or end of the clue.
  • Part 1 — a word or short synonym for the first chunk of letters.
  • Part 2 (…and more) — a word or short synonym for the next chunk. Keep going until the parts are used up.

No indicator word is required — juxtaposition (sometimes with "and") is often the only signal. The skill is spotting where the whole word can be cut into two or three smaller real words or abbreviations, each with its own plausible synonym.

Worked example

"Everyone was in debt — that's permitted (7)"

"That's permitted" is the definition, at the end. Split the rest into two pieces: "Everyone" and "was in debt." Everyone → ALL. Was in debt → OWED. Glue them in order:

ALL + OWED = ALLOWED

Seven letters, means "permitted." Both parts check out, both promises kept.

Practice

Find the cut point(s), define each piece, glue left to right.

"Vehicle and favourite make floor covering (6)"

Charade — find two small words for "Vehicle" and "favourite", then glue them left to right.
Vehicle → a 3-letter word for a car; favourite → a 3-letter word, as in "teacher's ___".
Definition: floor covering. Parts: Vehicle → CAR, favourite → PET (as in "teacher's pet"). CAR + PET = CARPET.

"Restaurant bill, allowed, is medicine (6)"

Charade — find two small words for "Restaurant bill" and "allowed", then glue them left to right.
Restaurant bill → a 3-letter word (as in "pick up the ___"); allowed → a 3-letter word, same as a rental agreement.
Definition: medicine. Parts: Restaurant bill → TAB, allowed → LET. TAB + LET = TABLET.

"Weapon used by many a Round Table knight (8)"

Charade — find words for "Weapon" and "many", then glue them left to right.
Weapon → a 5-letter pole weapon; many → a 3-letter word, as in "a ___ of".
Definition: Round Table knight. Parts: Weapon → LANCE, many → LOT. LANCE + LOT = LANCELOT. (Source example, Best For Puzzles.)
