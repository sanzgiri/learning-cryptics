Homophones

Every trick so far has worked on spelling — hiding, shuffling, gluing, splitting, flipping, trimming letters. Homophones are the first trick that abandons spelling entirely and works on sound instead.

The recipe

Per the Best For Puzzles tutorial, a homophone clue asks for "a word meaning [definition A] which sounds the same as a word meaning [definition B]." The fodder word and the answer are spelled differently, mean different things, but are pronounced identically. The indicator flags that you should listen, not spell:

  • so we hear, heard, so it's said, in speech, we hear, by the sound of it

Like double definitions, there's no fodder to shuffle or trim — the whole skill is lateral, synonym-and-sound hunting rather than letter manipulation.

Worked example

"Woodland tree, so we hear, is a coastal area (5)"

"A coastal area" is the definition, at the end. "So we hear" is the homophone indicator. "Woodland tree" — BEECH — sounds identical to BEACH, a coastal area. Different spelling, different meaning, same sound. Both promises kept.

Practice

Find the fodder's meaning, then think about what it sounds like rather than how it's spelled.

"Garden bloom, we hear, is used for baking (5)"

Indicator is "we hear" — think of a word that sounds like "Garden bloom" but is spelled differently.
Garden bloom sounds identical to the powder bakers use.
Definition: used for baking. Indicator: we hear. Fodder: FLOWER sounds like FLOUR.

"Male child, so it's said, lights up the sky (3)"

Indicator is "so it's said" — think of a word that sounds like "Male child".
Every family (that has one) has one; it sounds exactly like something in the sky.
Definition: lights up the sky. Indicator: so it's said. Fodder: SON sounds like SUN.

"Croaky-voiced, by the sound of it, is a stable animal (5)"

Indicator is "by the sound of it" — think of a word that sounds like "Croaky-voiced".
If you've lost your voice, you sound like this stable animal.
Definition: stable animal. Indicator: by the sound of it. Fodder: HOARSE sounds like HORSE.

Primary source

See the Best For Puzzles homophones tutorial for the fuller indicator list. One catch worth knowing early: homophone clues can be unreliable across accents — a pair that sounds identical in one setter's accent may not in yours. Don't blame yourself if a homophone clue in a real puzzle feels off; it's a known friction point, not just a beginner problem.
