Containers (Insertion)

Charades glued parts side by side, left to right. Containers do something sneakier: one part gets stuffed inside another, splitting it open.

The recipe

Per the Best For Puzzles tutorial, a container clue has two word-parts, same as a charade — but instead of concatenating them, one is split open and the other is inserted into the gap. The indicator word tells you which direction:

  • Container indicators (the outer word does the holding): holding, surrounding, swallowing, taking in.
  • Contents indicators (the inner word does the entering): within, filling, entering, tucked into.

Same underlying trick either way — you just need to know which part is the container (gets split open) and which is the contents (gets slotted inside).

Worked example

"Currently entering ruler is aware (7)"

"Is aware" is the definition, at the end (knowing = shrewdly aware, as in "a knowing look"). "Entering" is a contents-indicator, so "Currently" — NOW — goes into "ruler" — KING. Split KING open and slot NOW inside:

K + NOW + ING = KNOWING

Seven letters, means "aware." Both promises kept.

Practice

Work out which part is the container and which is the contents before you try to slot anything in.

"Boy held by Mary makes stonework (7)"

Container — "held by" tells you which part wraps around which. Find a name that means "Mary" and a small word for "Boy".
Mary splits into MA + RY; Boy → a 3-letter word, as in someone's SON.
Definition: stonework. "held by" is a container indicator — Mary (container) holds Boy → SON (contents). MA + SON + RY = MASONRY.

"Bird: allowed, holding tavern (6)"

Container — "holding" tells you which part wraps around which. Find a word for "allowed" and a word for "tavern".
allowed → a 3-letter word, as on a rental sign; tavern → a 3-letter word for a small pub.
Definition: Bird. "holding" is a container indicator — allowed → LET (container) holds tavern → INN (contents). L + INN + ET = LINNET.
