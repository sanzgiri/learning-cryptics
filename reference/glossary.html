Cryptic Crosswords — Glossary

Canonical terms for this workspace, added only once you've actually used them. Built up lesson by lesson — see Lesson 1.

Definition
The plain, ordinary synonym for the answer, sitting at the very start or very end of a clue — never in the middle.
Wordplay
The coded recipe, built from the rest of the clue, that constructs the answer letter by letter through some trick (e.g. a hidden word).
Enumeration
The number(s) in brackets at the end of a clue, e.g. (5) or (4,3) — the letter count of the answer, and how it splits across words. A hard constraint, not a meaning hint.
Hidden word (clue type)
A wordplay trick where the answer is spelled out by consecutive letters running straight through a stretch of the clue, ignoring word breaks.
Hidden-word indicator
A word signalling "contained within," marking a hidden-word clue — e.g. in, within, part of, some of, hidden in, held by, found in.
Not exhaustive — more indicators will turn up as you solve more puzzles.
Fodder
The letters in a clue that wordplay actually operates on — hunted through for a hidden word, or shuffled for an anagram. Everything in the clue that isn't the definition or an indicator.
Anagram (clue type)
A wordplay trick where the fodder's letters are rearranged, exactly and completely, to form the answer. See Lesson 2.
Anagram indicator
A word suggesting disorder, movement, or transformation, marking an anagram clue — e.g. broken, mixed, chaotic, dancing, wild, reformed, acting up, oddly, at sea. Not a fixed list — setters invent new ones constantly.
Double definition (clue type)
A clue with no wordplay indicator at all — just two distinct senses of the same word, placed back to back, each independently defining the answer. See Lesson 3.
The two senses must be genuinely distinct — two synonyms for the same sense (e.g. "sort, variety" for KIND) isn't a valid double definition.
Charade (clue type)
A clue built from two or more parts, each separately defined, concatenated in order (left to right, no shuffling, no hiding) to form the answer. See Lesson 4.
Container (clue type)
A clue with two parts, like a charade, except one part is split open and the other is inserted inside it rather than placed alongside it. See Lesson 5.
Container indicator
A word signalling the outer part does the holding — e.g. holding, surrounding, swallowing, taking in.
Contents indicator
A word signalling the inner part does the entering — e.g. within, filling, entering, tucked into.
Reversal (clue type)
A wordplay trick where a fodder word, read backwards letter by letter, spells the answer (or part of it). See Lesson 6.
Reversal indicator
A word suggesting turning or flipping, marking a reversal clue — e.g. going round, turned, backward, reversed, overturned, sent back. In down clues, indicators often suggest "upward" instead (e.g. rising, held up), since reversing a vertical word means reading it bottom to top.
Subtraction (clue type)
A wordplay trick where one or more letters are removed from a fodder word, leaving the answer. The mirror image of a container clue. See Lesson 7.
Subtraction indicator
A word signalling which letters to remove and from where — e.g. beheaded, first off (drop the start), heartless, hollow (drop the middle), incomplete, endless (drop the end), without limits, lacking wings (drop both ends).
Bits and pieces (clue type)
A wordplay trick where a single letter — not a whole word — is extracted from a fodder word via a positional phrase, then used as one piece of a charade. See Lesson 8.
Positional letter indicator
A phrase pointing at a single letter inside the word that follows it — e.g. head of X / start of X (first letter), heart of X (middle letter), end of X / conclusion of X (last letter). "Initially" applied to several words in a row means "take the first letter of each."
Homophone (clue type)
A wordplay trick where a fodder word, spelled and meaning something different, is pronounced identically to the answer. The first trick that works on sound rather than spelling. See Lesson 9.
Can be unreliable across accents — a pair that's a perfect homophone for one setter may not be for every solver.
Homophone indicator
A word flagging that you should listen rather than spell — e.g. so we hear, heard, so it's said, in speech, we hear, by the sound of it.
Cryptic definition (clue type)
A clue with a single definition, deliberately phrased to mislead — no separate wordplay half to fall back on. Distinct from a double definition, which gives two genuine, separate definitions. See Lesson 10.
No fixed indicator word — the giveaway is structural (no anagram/hidden/reversal-style indicator anywhere), often paired with a trailing question mark.
Complex clue (clue type)
A clue combining two or more wordplay tricks from earlier lessons in a single clue (e.g. a reversal feeding into a charade). Not a new mechanic — a synthesis of everything already learned. See Lesson 11.
&Lit clue (clue type)
Short for "and literally so." A clue with no separate definition at all — the entire clue functions simultaneously as the wordplay and as a plain, honest definition of the answer. See Lesson 12.
Rare and hard even for experienced setters to construct well — don't expect to spot one in every grid.
