Mission: Solving Cryptic Crosswords

Why

You want to be able to sit down with a British-style cryptic crossword (Guardian/Times) and actually solve it — not just admire clues after seeing the answer. You're starting from zero: no familiarity yet with clue types or cryptic conventions.

See Curriculum for the full ordered list of clue types, what's done, and what's left. After Lesson 12, Drill: Mixed Practice is hundreds of real clues per type.

Success looks like

  • Recognize the major clue types on sight (anagram, hidden word, double definition, charade, container/insertion, homophone, reversal, deletion) and identify which is in play for a given clue.
  • Parse a clue into definition + wordplay, and explain why an answer works, not just guess it.
  • Complete an easy/beginner-level British cryptic (e.g. a Guardian "Quiptic" or Times Quick Cryptic) with minimal help.
  • Work up to a standard daily Guardian or Times cryptic with occasional help.

Constraints

  • Complete beginner — no prior cryptic-solving experience.
  • Learning happens in short sessions over time (stateful, multi-session).

Out of scope

  • Advanced/specialist puzzles (Listener, barred-grid, cryptic setting/constructing) — revisit only after daily cryptics are comfortable.
  • American-style (NYT/New Yorker) conventions — focus is British-style for now (see the American vs. British comparison on the Drill page if curious).
