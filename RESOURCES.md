# Cryptic Crosswords Resources

## Knowledge

- [The Guardian: "How to solve cryptic crosswords — the ultimate beginner's guide"](https://www.theguardian.com/crosswords/crossword-blog/2024/oct/21/how-to-solve-cryptic-crosswords-the-ultimate-beginners-guide)
  Guardian crossword editors' own beginner guide. Covers the core clue types, points to the Quiptic and Quick Cryptic as beginner puzzles, and links the "Meet the Setter" series. Use for: first orientation, and as the primary citation for early lessons.
- [Fifteensquared](https://www.fifteensquared.net/)
  Long-running blog (est. mid-2000s) that publishes a full parsing of every Guardian, Independent, and Guardian Quick Cryptic puzzle the day it's set, with definitions underlined and wordplay explained. Use for: checking your parsing against an expert's after attempting a puzzle; has a beginner-friendly glossary of setter abbreviations and clue-type jargon.
- [Times for the Times](https://timesforthetimes.co.uk/)
  Equivalent blog for The Times Daily Cryptic, Quick Cryptic, and Saturday Cryptic — started by a two-time Times Crossword Championship winner, now run by a wider team. Use for: parsing help once you move from Guardian-style to Times-style clues (Times cluing is stricter/more Ximenean).
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Dictionary — Anagram Indicators](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-dictionary/anagram-indicators.html)
  Reference list of anagram-indicator words/phrases (disorder, movement, alteration, mental-state, and phrase indicators) with clue-structure explanation. Use for: anagram-clue lessons and as a lookup when an unfamiliar indicator word shows up in a real puzzle.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Double Definitions](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/double-definitions.html)
  Explains the double-definition clue type: two distinct senses of one word, no wordplay indicator, must read as coherent surface English. Includes validated example clues. Use for: double-definition lessons.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Charades](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/charades.html)
  Explains charade clues: word split into parts, each independently defined, concatenated left-to-right (no shuffling, no hiding). Confirmed example: ALL (everyone) + OWED (was in debt) = ALLOWED. Use for: charade-clue lessons. The wider tutorial index at bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/ covers the remaining clue types (homophones, reversals, containers, subtraction, & lit) — go there next for lesson 5+.
  Note: one example fetched from this page (a "CARRIAGE" clue) didn't check out letter-for-letter when verified by hand — treat any single example from a fetched summary as unverified until manually letter-counted before use in a lesson.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Container & Contents Clues](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/containers.html)
  Explains insertion clues: one word's letters placed inside another's, split at some point. Indicator words split into "container" type (holding, surrounding, swallowing, taking in) vs. "contents" type (within, filling, entering, tucked into). Confirmed by hand: KNOWING = K + NOW + ING (NOW inside KING), MASONRY = MA + SON + RY (SON inside MARY). Use for: container/insertion clue lessons.
  Note: this page's own worked "Example 1–3" clues (LINNET, GARMENT, ADHERED) did NOT check out letter-for-letter on manual verification — same fetch-summarization unreliability as the charades page. Only the prose-described KNOWING/MASONRY examples were trustworthy; any answer built independently from those (e.g. a hand-verified LINNET = L + INN + ET) is original work, not sourced.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Index](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/)
  Full ordered index of every clue-type tutorial page (double definitions, cryptic definitions, anagrams, hidden words, homophones, reversals, charades, containers, bits and pieces, subtraction, complex clues, & lit clues). Use for: confirming the canonical list and order behind [Curriculum](curriculum.html).
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Reversals](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/reversals.html)
  Explains reversal clues: fodder word read backwards to form the answer. Indicators: going round, turned, backward, reversed, overturned. Use for: reversal-clue lessons.
  Note: this page's own worked example ("stronghold" → reversal) did not check out letter-for-letter — same unreliability pattern. All Lesson 6 practice examples (NET/TEN, POTS/STOP, STRAW/WARTS) are original, hand-verified work, not sourced.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Subtraction](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/subtraction.html)
  Explains subtraction clues: letters removed from a fodder word to leave the answer. Indicators grouped by position: beheaded/first off (start), heartless/hollow (middle), incomplete/endless (end), without limits/lacking wings (both ends). Concept confirmed reliable on this fetch; Lesson 7 examples (PARK/SPARK, CHIN/CHINA, SHUT/SHOUT, TAR/STARS) are original, hand-verified work.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Bits and Pieces](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/bits-and-pieces.html)
  Explains single-letter extraction: "head of X" = first letter, "heart of X" = middle letter, "end of X"/"conclusion of X" = last letter, plus common abbreviations (compass points, Roman numerals, chemical symbols). Fetched examples (Head of state = S, Heart of stone = O, Conclusion of journey = Y, Land's End = D, Outskirts of Liverpool = LL) were checked by hand and all confirmed correct — first source page where the fetch held up cleanly on letter-level detail. Lesson 8 practice clues (OPEN, DRUM, SOFA) are still original, hand-verified constructions.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Homophones](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/homophones.html)
  Explains homophone clues and lists indicators: so we hear, heard, so it's said, in speech, we hear, by the sound of it. Use for: homophone-clue lessons. Low letter-verification risk since homophones hinge on pronunciation, not spelling reconstruction.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Cryptic Definitions](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/cryptic-definitions.html)
  Explains cryptic definitions: a single definition merged with misdirection, no separate wordplay half. Distinguishes from double definitions. Source's own example (RUBBER BAND for "A pop group?") is a well-known public-domain crossword convention, verified by letter count. Use for: cryptic-definition lessons.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — Complex Clues](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/complex-clues.html)
  Explains complex clues as a synthesis of two or more prior mechanisms in one clue, not a new trick. Use for: framing Lesson 11 as review/combination rather than new content.
- [Best For Puzzles: Cryptic Crossword Tutorial — &Lit Clues](https://bestforpuzzles.com/cryptic-crossword-tutorial/and-lit.html)
  Explains &lit ("and literally so") clues: the whole clue functions as both wordplay and definition, no separate definition part. Source's VILE/"Terribly evil" example verified by hand (EVIL anagrams exactly to VILE) and used directly as Lesson 12's worked example. Practice clues (OUTLAW, INDOORS) are original constructions — the tutorial itself notes genuine &lit clues are hard to construct, which is reflected in the lesson's honesty about their rarity.

- [cryptics.georgeho.org](https://cryptics.georgeho.org/) — "A Dataset of Cryptic Crossword Clues"
  Open dataset (ODbL license) of 660,000+ real clues scraped from Fifteensquared, Big Dave 44, Times for the Times, National Post, Hindu Crossword Corner, and other blogs, with clue text, answer, extracted definition span, and (via `indicators_by_clue`/`charades_by_clue` tables) machine-tagged wordplay mechanism for ~8 of the 12 clue types. Use for: this is the source of every clue in `drill/data.js` — real, published, community-vetted content, not generated. Queried via its public Datasette SQL JSON API (see `drill/` build notes); double definitions and cryptic definitions were pulled via a structural heuristic (definition field containing "/" for double-defs; "?"/"!"-terminated clues where the definition spans most of the clue for cryptic-defs and &lit) rather than an explicit tag, since the dataset doesn't tag those three types directly — spot-checked by hand, not exhaustively verified given the volume (3,840 clues total).
- [Daily Cryptic's solver](https://www.dailycryptic.org/cryptic-crossword-solver)
  Search by clue text, answer, or letter pattern; includes a 1,600+ word indicator directory. Use for: looking up an unfamiliar indicator word encountered in a real puzzle.
- [Leftfield Cryptics constructors' glossary](https://www.leftfieldcryptics.com/glossary.html)
  Setter-side terminology reference. Use for: once conventions start being noticed rather than just decoded.

## American vs. British cryptics

Per [Wikipedia's Cryptic crossword article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptic_crossword) (fetched 2026-07-19): American cryptics (rare — mainly *GAMES* magazine, *The Nation*, occasional *New Yorker* puzzles) hold to stricter construction rules than British ones — every word must serve the wordplay or definition, and a clue is generally allowed only one wordplay device (no [[complex clues]]). Double definitions and cryptic definitions are common in British/Canadian cryptics but essentially unused in American ones. Within British cryptics, *The Times* leans Ximenean (strict grammatical precision in indicators, per Ximenes' 1966 *Ximenes on the Art of the Crossword Puzzle*) while *The Guardian* leans libertarian (more relaxed). Cryptics are mainstream daily fare in Britain and common in Canada, India, and Australia, but niche in the US.
